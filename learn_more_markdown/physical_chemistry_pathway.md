


# Physical Chemistry

## Quantum Mechanical Description of Molecules

Physical chemistry treats atoms and molecules as quantum systems governed by wavefunctions. Electronic structure determines bonding, reactivity, and energy levels through solutions to the Schrödinger equation.

--- PAGE ---

## Statistical Mechanics and Ensemble Theory

Instead of tracking individual particles, statistical mechanics models large collections of molecules using probability distributions. Macroscopic properties emerge from ensemble averages over microscopic states.

--- PAGE ---

## Thermodynamics and State Functions

Thermodynamics describes energy, entropy, and free energy as state functions independent of path. Equilibrium behavior is determined by minimization of Gibbs free energy under constraints.

--- PAGE ---

## Chemical Potential and Phase Equilibrium

Chemical potential governs how species distribute across phases and reactions. Phase equilibrium occurs when chemical potential is equalized across components and phases.

--- PAGE ---

## Reaction Kinetics and Transition State Theory

Reaction rates are modeled using energy barriers and transition states. The Arrhenius equation and transition state theory describe how temperature and activation energy control reaction speed.

--- PAGE ---

## Molecular Energy Levels and Spectroscopy

Molecules have quantized vibrational, rotational, and electronic energy levels. Spectroscopy measures transitions between these levels to infer structure and dynamics.

--- PAGE ---

## Partition Functions and Thermodynamic Prediction

The partition function encodes all accessible energy states of a system. From it, macroscopic thermodynamic quantities like entropy and heat capacity can be derived.

--- PAGE ---

## Electrochemistry and Redox Potentials

Electrochemical systems involve electron transfer reactions governed by potential differences. The Nernst equation relates chemical concentrations to electrical potential.

--- PAGE ---

## Diffusion and Transport Equations

Molecular motion through space is described by diffusion equations and Fick’s laws. Transport phenomena link gradients in concentration, temperature, and pressure to flux.

--- PAGE ---

## Surface Chemistry and Adsorption Models

Reactions at surfaces depend on adsorption, binding energies, and surface coverage. Models like Langmuir isotherms describe how molecules interact with interfaces.

--- PAGE ---

## Non-Equilibrium Thermodynamics

Many systems operate far from equilibrium, requiring extended thermodynamic frameworks. These systems exhibit fluxes, gradients, and irreversible processes.

--- PAGE ---

## Molecular Dynamics and Computational Simulation

Molecular behavior can be simulated numerically using force fields and Newtonian mechanics at atomic scale. This connects classical physics with statistical predictions.

--- PAGE ---

## Free Energy Landscapes and Reaction Pathways

Chemical systems evolve across multidimensional energy landscapes. Reaction pathways follow minimum energy routes between stable and unstable states.

--- PAGE ---

## Phase Diagrams and Multicomponent Systems

Phase diagrams map stability regions of different physical states. Multicomponent systems require high-dimensional equilibrium analysis.

--- PAGE ---

## Fluctuations and Thermal Noise

At microscopic scales, systems exhibit random thermal fluctuations. These deviations from average behavior are modeled probabilistically rather than deterministically.
