


# Genetics

## DNA Structure and Base Pairing

DNA is the molecular code that stores genetic information in living organisms. It is composed of nucleotide bases (adenine, thymine, cytosine, and guanine) that pair in specific ways to form a double helix structure. The sequence of these bases encodes instructions for building proteins. This is essentially a biological information system with a fixed symbolic alphabet.

## Genes and Protein Coding

Genes are segments of DNA that contain instructions for producing proteins. Proteins carry out most of the functional work in cells, from structural roles to chemical reactions. The relationship between genes and proteins is governed by transcription and translation processes. This creates a step-by-step mapping from genetic code to biological function.

## Mendelian Inheritance

Mendelian genetics describes how traits are passed from parents to offspring using discrete units called alleles. Traits can be dominant or recessive, affecting how they appear in offspring. Probability is used to predict inheritance patterns using tools like Punnett squares. This introduces structured randomness into biological systems.

## Genetic Variation and Mutation

Mutations are changes in DNA sequences that can occur randomly or due to environmental factors. These changes create genetic variation, which is essential for evolution. Some mutations have no effect, while others can significantly alter traits or disease risk. This introduces stochastic processes into biological systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Probability in Genetic Outcomes

Genetic inheritance is fundamentally probabilistic rather than deterministic. The combination of alleles from two parents creates a range of possible outcomes with different likelihoods. Population genetics uses statistical models to predict trait distribution over generations. This connects genetics directly to probability theory.

## Natural Selection and Evolutionary Pressure

Natural selection is the process by which traits that improve survival or reproduction become more common over time. Environmental pressures shape which genetic variations persist in a population. This creates a feedback system between genetics and environmental conditions. It is effectively an optimization process across generations.

## Genotype vs Phenotype Mapping

The genotype is the underlying genetic code, while the phenotype is the observable expression of traits. The mapping between the two is complex and influenced by environment, gene interactions, and regulatory systems. Small genetic changes can sometimes produce large phenotypic effects. This introduces nonlinear relationships into biological modeling.

## Gene Expression and Regulation

Not all genes are active at all times; gene expression is regulated depending on cellular conditions. Regulatory systems control when and how strongly genes are turned on or off. This allows cells with identical DNA to behave differently. It is a dynamic control system operating on genetic information.

## Chromosomes and Genetic Organization

DNA is organized into chromosomes, which package and structure genetic material inside cells. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes, each containing many genes. The arrangement of genetic material affects inheritance and recombination. This introduces hierarchical structure into biological information systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Population Genetics and Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

Population genetics studies how gene frequencies change across groups over time. The Hardy-Weinberg principle describes conditions under which allele frequencies remain stable. Deviations from this model indicate evolutionary forces like selection, mutation, or migration. This introduces equilibrium modeling into biology.

--- PAGE ---

## Genetic Drift and Random Sampling Effects

Genetic drift refers to random changes in allele frequencies, especially in small populations. Unlike natural selection, drift is driven by chance rather than fitness advantages. Over time, it can significantly alter genetic composition. This introduces randomness as a structural force in evolution.

## Modern Genetic Engineering and CRISPR

Genetic engineering allows scientists to directly modify DNA sequences using tools like CRISPR-Cas9. This technology enables precise editing of genes for research, agriculture, and medicine. It transforms genetics from a descriptive science into a controllable system. This introduces direct manipulation of biological information.
