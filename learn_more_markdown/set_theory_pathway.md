


# Set Theory

## Sets and Membership Relations

A set is a collection of distinct objects, and membership defines whether an element belongs to a set. This simple relation forms the basis for nearly all mathematical structures.

--- PAGE ---

## Axiomatic Set Theory and ZFC Foundations

Modern set theory is built on axioms such as Zermelo–Fraenkel with the Axiom of Choice (ZFC). These rules define how sets can be formed and manipulated without paradoxes.

--- PAGE ---

## Subsets and Power Sets

A subset contains elements entirely within another set. The power set of a set is the collection of all its possible subsets, leading to exponential growth in structure complexity.

--- PAGE ---

## Set Operations and Algebra of Collections

Union, intersection, and complement define operations on sets. These operations form an algebraic system that mirrors logical and probabilistic structure.

--- PAGE ---

## Cartesian Products and Ordered Structure

The Cartesian product constructs ordered pairs or tuples from sets. This introduces structure where order and dimensionality become meaningful.

--- PAGE ---

## Relations and Equivalence Classes

Relations define how elements of sets are connected. Equivalence relations partition sets into classes of structurally similar elements.

--- PAGE ---

## Functions as Special Relations

Functions are relations where each input has exactly one output. They serve as fundamental building blocks for all mathematical mappings and transformations.

--- PAGE ---

## Cardinality and Infinite Size Comparisons

Cardinality measures the size of sets, including infinite sets. Different infinities can be compared, revealing surprising hierarchies of infinite magnitude.

--- PAGE ---

## Countable and Uncountable Infinities

Some infinite sets, like integers, are countable, while others, like real numbers, are uncountable. This distinction shows that not all infinities are equal.

--- PAGE ---

## Cantor’s Diagonal Argument

Cantor’s argument proves that real numbers cannot be listed in a complete sequence. It demonstrates the existence of strictly larger infinities.

--- PAGE ---

## Ordinal Numbers and Order Structure

Ordinals extend numbers to describe ordering types beyond finite sequences. They capture structure in well-ordered sets, including infinite ordering systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Cardinal Arithmetic and Infinite Operations

Set theory defines arithmetic for infinite sizes. Operations on cardinal numbers behave differently from finite arithmetic, revealing deep structural differences.

--- PAGE ---

## Axiom of Choice and Selection Principles

The Axiom of Choice allows selection of elements from infinitely many sets without a specific rule. It leads to powerful results but also counterintuitive consequences.

--- PAGE ---

## Zorn’s Lemma and Maximal Structures

Zorn’s Lemma guarantees the existence of maximal elements under certain conditions. It is equivalent to the Axiom of Choice and widely used in algebra and analysis.

--- PAGE ---

## Well-Ordering Theorem and Global Ordering

Every set can be well-ordered under certain axiomatic assumptions. This means every set has a structure where every subset has a least element.

--- PAGE ---

## Foundations of Mathematics and Structural Reduction

Set theory provides a foundation for defining numbers, functions, spaces, and logic itself. Almost all mathematical objects can be encoded as sets.
