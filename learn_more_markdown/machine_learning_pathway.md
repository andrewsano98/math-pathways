


# Basic Machine Learning Techniques

## Linear Regression

A basic machine learning technique that models the relationship between input variables and a continuous output using a best-fit line (or hyperplane). It minimizes prediction error across data points.

--- PAGE ---

## Logistic Regression

Despite its name, this is a classification method that predicts probabilities of discrete outcomes (usually binary). It uses a sigmoid function to map linear combinations into probability values.

--- PAGE ---

## Decision Trees

A model that splits data into branches based on feature thresholds. Each split reduces uncertainty until a final prediction is made at the leaf nodes.

--- PAGE ---

## Random Forests

An ensemble method that combines many decision trees trained on different subsets of data. Final predictions are made by aggregating outputs, reducing overfitting and improving stability.

--- PAGE ---

## Support Vector Machines (SVM)

A classification technique that finds the optimal boundary (hyperplane) separating classes with the largest margin. It can also use kernel functions to handle nonlinear data.

--- PAGE ---

## K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

A simple algorithm that classifies new data points based on the majority class of their nearest neighbors in feature space. It relies heavily on distance metrics.

--- PAGE ---

## Naive Bayes Classifier

A probabilistic classifier based on Bayes’ theorem with an assumption of feature independence. It is especially effective in text classification tasks.

--- PAGE ---

## Clustering (K-Means)

An unsupervised learning method that groups data into clusters by minimizing distance between points and their assigned cluster centers.

--- PAGE ---

## Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

A dimensionality reduction technique that transforms data into a smaller set of uncorrelated variables while preserving as much variance as possible.

--- PAGE ---

## Neural Networks

Computational models inspired by the brain, consisting of layers of interconnected nodes. They learn complex patterns by adjusting weighted connections during training.

--- PAGE ---

## Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

A specialized type of neural network designed for spatial data like images. They use filters to detect patterns such as edges, textures, and shapes.

--- PAGE ---

## Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

Networks designed for sequential data. They maintain memory of previous inputs, making them useful for time series and language processing.

--- PAGE ---

## Gradient Descent

An optimization algorithm used to minimize error by iteratively adjusting model parameters in the direction that reduces loss.

--- PAGE ---

## Overfitting and Regularization

Overfitting occurs when a model learns noise instead of general patterns. Regularization techniques (like L1/L2 penalties or dropout) help prevent this.

--- PAGE ---

## Train/Test Split and Model Evaluation

Data is divided into training and testing sets to evaluate how well a model generalizes to unseen data. Performance metrics measure accuracy, precision, recall, and more.

--- PAGE ---

## Feature Engineering

The process of selecting, transforming, or creating input variables that improve model performance. It is often as important as the model itself.

--- PAGE ---

## Ensemble Methods

Techniques that combine multiple models to improve accuracy and robustness. Examples include bagging, boosting, and stacking.
