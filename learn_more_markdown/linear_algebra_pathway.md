


# Linear Algebra

## Vector Spaces and Linear Structure

A vector space is a collection of objects that can be added together and scaled by numbers while preserving structure. This abstraction allows geometry, physics, and data to be treated uniformly in higher dimensions.

--- PAGE ---

## Linear Independence and Basis Systems

A basis is a minimal set of vectors that can represent every element in a space through linear combinations. Linear independence ensures no redundancy, forming the coordinate foundation of vector spaces.

--- PAGE ---

## Linear Transformations and Structure Preservation

Linear transformations map vectors between spaces while preserving addition and scalar multiplication. They represent structured changes such as rotations, scalings, and projections.

--- PAGE ---

## Matrices as Computational Representations

Matrices encode linear transformations in a concrete form. They allow abstract transformations to be computed efficiently and are central to numerical computation and applied mathematics.

--- PAGE ---

## Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Eigenvectors are directions that remain unchanged under a linear transformation, while eigenvalues describe the scaling factor. They reveal intrinsic structure within transformations.

--- PAGE ---

## Diagonalization and Simplification of Systems

Diagonalization expresses matrices in a simplified form using eigenbases. This reduces complex transformations into independent scaling operations along principal directions.

--- PAGE ---

## Determinants and Volume Scaling

The determinant measures how a linear transformation scales volume and whether it preserves orientation. It provides global structural information about transformations.

--- PAGE ---

## Inner Product Spaces and Geometry

Inner products generalize dot products and define notions of length, angle, and orthogonality. This connects algebraic structure with geometric interpretation.

--- PAGE ---

## Orthogonality and Projection

Orthogonal vectors are independent in a geometric sense. Projections decompose vectors into components along subspaces, enabling approximation and decomposition techniques.

--- PAGE ---

## Gram–Schmidt Process and Orthonormal Bases

The Gram–Schmidt process converts a set of vectors into an orthonormal basis. This provides a structured coordinate system for simplifying computations.

--- PAGE ---

## Rank and Dimension Theory

Rank measures the number of independent directions in a matrix or transformation. It reveals the effective dimensionality of linear systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Systems of Linear Equations

Linear systems are solved using matrix operations, representing constraints in vector form. Solutions depend on consistency, rank, and geometric interpretation of intersections.

--- PAGE ---

## Null Space and Kernel Structure

The null space contains all vectors mapped to zero by a transformation. It captures loss of information and structural degeneracy in linear systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)

SVD decomposes matrices into orthogonal transformations and scaling components. It reveals deep structure in data, enabling compression and noise reduction.

--- PAGE ---

## Spectral Theory and Operator Structure

Spectral theory generalizes eigenvalue analysis to infinite-dimensional spaces. It studies how operators act through their spectral properties.

--- PAGE ---

## Applications in Data and Computation

Linear algebra underlies machine learning, graphics, physics simulations, and optimization. High-dimensional data is interpreted as geometry in vector spaces.
