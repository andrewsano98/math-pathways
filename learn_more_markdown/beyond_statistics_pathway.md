


# Beyond Statistics

## Probability Spaces and Measure-Theoretic Foundations

Advanced statistics is built on probability spaces defined rigorously using measure theory. This formalizes randomness as a structured mathematical object rather than intuition about chance.

--- PAGE ---

## Law of Large Numbers and Asymptotic Stability

The law of large numbers states that averages of random variables converge to expected values as sample size increases. This provides stability in large-scale inference.

--- PAGE ---

## Central Limit Theorem and Universal Normality

The central limit theorem shows that sums of independent random variables tend toward a normal distribution. This explains why Gaussian models appear across many domains.

--- PAGE ---

## Statistical Estimation and Parameter Inference

Estimation theory focuses on inferring unknown parameters from observed data. Methods like maximum likelihood estimation and Bayesian inference quantify uncertainty in estimates.

--- PAGE ---

## Bayesian Inference and Prior–Posterior Updating

Bayesian statistics models knowledge as probability distributions that are updated with new data. Priors represent initial beliefs, and posteriors represent updated knowledge after evidence.

--- PAGE ---

## Hypothesis Testing and Decision Thresholds

Hypothesis testing evaluates competing claims using statistical evidence. Significance levels, p-values, and test statistics control false positive and false negative rates.

--- PAGE ---

## Confidence Intervals and Uncertainty Quantification

Confidence intervals provide ranges of plausible parameter values. They quantify uncertainty rather than giving single-point estimates.

--- PAGE ---

## Regression Models and Functional Relationships

Regression analyzes relationships between variables by fitting functions to data. Linear and nonlinear models capture dependencies and predictive structure.

--- PAGE ---

## Generalized Linear Models and Link Functions

GLMs extend linear regression to non-normal data distributions using link functions. This allows modeling of categorical, count, and bounded outcomes.

--- PAGE ---

## Multivariate Statistics and High-Dimensional Data

Multivariate analysis studies systems with many variables simultaneously. Covariance structure and correlation matrices describe dependencies in high-dimensional spaces.

--- PAGE ---

## Principal Component Analysis and Dimensional Reduction

PCA identifies directions of maximum variance in data. It reduces dimensionality while preserving as much structural information as possible.

--- PAGE ---

## Markov Chains and Stochastic Processes

Markov models describe systems that evolve probabilistically over time. Future states depend only on the current state, not full history.

--- PAGE ---

## Monte Carlo Methods and Numerical Simulation

Monte Carlo methods use random sampling to approximate complex integrals or distributions. They are essential in high-dimensional computation and uncertainty estimation.

--- PAGE ---

## Statistical Learning Theory and Generalization

Learning theory studies how models generalize from finite data to unseen cases. It formalizes overfitting, capacity, and sample complexity.

--- PAGE ---

## Causal Inference and Structural Models

Causal inference distinguishes correlation from causation using structured assumptions and intervention-based models. It uses graphs and counterfactual reasoning.

--- PAGE ---

## Information Theory and Entropy-Based Statistics

Entropy measures uncertainty in distributions. Information theory connects probability with compression, learning efficiency, and statistical inference limits.
