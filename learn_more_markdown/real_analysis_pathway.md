


# Real Analysis

## Real Numbers and Completeness

Real analysis begins with the structure of the real number system, defined as a complete ordered field. Completeness ensures that every bounded sequence has a limit within the real numbers, preventing “gaps” in the system.

--- PAGE ---

## Limits and Epsilon–Delta Formalism

Limits are defined rigorously using ε–δ language. A function approaches a limit if values can be made arbitrarily close by restricting inputs sufficiently, formalizing the idea of convergence without intuition.

--- PAGE ---

## Continuity and Local Stability

A function is continuous if small changes in input produce small changes in output. In real analysis, continuity is defined entirely through limits, ensuring precise control over local behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Sequences and Convergence Criteria

Sequences are ordered lists of numbers whose behavior is studied as they progress to infinity. Convergence depends on whether values approach a single fixed point under strict metric conditions.

--- PAGE ---

## Cauchy Sequences and Internal Consistency

A sequence is Cauchy if its terms become arbitrarily close to each other. In complete spaces like the real numbers, every Cauchy sequence converges, linking internal consistency to existence of limits.

--- PAGE ---

## Uniform Continuity and Global Control

Uniform continuity strengthens standard continuity by requiring a single δ to work across the entire domain. This ensures consistent behavior regardless of location in the input space.

--- PAGE ---

## Compactness and Finite Substructure Behavior

Compact sets behave like finite systems in analysis. Every open cover has a finite subcover, enabling strong results about continuity, convergence, and boundedness.

--- PAGE ---

## Bolzano–Weierstrass Theorem

Every bounded sequence in the real numbers has a convergent subsequence. This result guarantees structure within infinite sequences and is foundational for compactness arguments.

--- PAGE ---

## Monotone Convergence Theorem

Every bounded monotone sequence converges. This connects order structure with limiting behavior and provides a powerful tool for proving convergence without explicit computation.

--- PAGE ---

## Pointwise vs Uniform Convergence

Pointwise convergence tracks limits at individual points, while uniform convergence ensures consistent convergence across entire domains. The distinction is critical for interchanging limits and integrals.

--- PAGE ---

## Riemann Integration and Area Construction

Integration is defined as a limit of partitions of intervals. The Riemann integral formalizes area under a curve using sums of increasingly fine approximations.

--- PAGE ---

## Lebesgue Measure and Generalized Integration

Lebesgue integration extends the concept of integration by measuring sets of points rather than intervals. This allows integration of more complex and irregular functions.

--- PAGE ---

## Metric Spaces and Generalized Distance

Real analysis extends beyond real numbers into metric spaces, where distance is defined abstractly. This allows convergence and continuity to be studied in general settings.

--- PAGE ---

## Fixed Point Theorems in Analysis

Fixed point results guarantee that certain functions must have points where input equals output under specific conditions. These theorems are foundational in analysis and applied mathematics.

--- PAGE ---

## Functional Limits and Operator Behavior

Real analysis also studies limits of function sequences and operators. This leads to functional analysis, where functions themselves are treated as points in higher-dimensional spaces.
