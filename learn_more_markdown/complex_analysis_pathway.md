


# Complex Analysis

## Complex Numbers and the Complex Plane

Complex analysis begins by extending real numbers to include imaginary components. Complex numbers are represented on a 2D plane, where arithmetic gains geometric interpretation through rotations and scaling.

--- PAGE ---

## Analytic Functions and Differentiability

A function is analytic if it is complex-differentiable at every point in a region. This is a much stronger condition than real differentiability and leads to highly constrained and structured behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Cauchy–Riemann Equations

The Cauchy–Riemann equations determine whether a complex function is differentiable. They link partial derivatives of real and imaginary components, enforcing strict consistency conditions on the function.

--- PAGE ---

## Complex Integration and Contour Paths

Integration in complex analysis is performed along paths in the complex plane. The value of these integrals depends on the function’s structure and the chosen contour, not just endpoints.

--- PAGE ---

## Cauchy’s Integral Theorem

If a function is analytic within a region, its integral around any closed loop is zero. This result reveals deep conservation-like properties in complex systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Cauchy’s Integral Formula

This formula allows the value of an analytic function inside a region to be determined entirely by its values on the boundary. It is a powerful reconstruction principle.

--- PAGE ---

## Laurent Series and Singularities

Functions with singular points can be expanded into Laurent series, which include both positive and negative powers. This reveals detailed local behavior near discontinuities.

--- PAGE ---

## Residue Theorem and Complex Integration Evaluation

The residue theorem allows complex integrals to be computed by analyzing singularities. It transforms difficult integrals into discrete sums of local contributions.

--- PAGE ---

## Poles, Essential Singularities, and Behavior Classification

Singularities are classified based on how functions behave near undefined points. Poles are structured divergences, while essential singularities exhibit chaotic local behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Conformal Mappings and Angle Preservation

Conformal maps preserve angles while transforming shapes. These mappings are used to simplify complex geometric or physical problems while retaining local structure.

--- PAGE ---

## Complex Exponentials and Euler’s Formula

Euler’s formula links exponential and trigonometric functions through complex numbers. It reveals deep periodic structure in exponential growth and rotation.

--- PAGE ---

## Harmonic Functions and Laplace’s Equation

The real and imaginary parts of analytic functions satisfy Laplace’s equation, connecting complex analysis to physics, especially in steady-state systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Analytic Continuation and Function Extension

Analytic continuation extends functions beyond their original domain while preserving consistency. This allows global understanding from local information.

--- PAGE ---

## Riemann Surfaces and Multivalued Functions

Some complex functions are naturally defined on layered surfaces rather than single planes. Riemann surfaces resolve multivalued behavior into structured geometry.

--- PAGE ---

## Applications in Physics and Engineering

Complex analysis appears in fluid flow, electromagnetism, and wave theory, where it simplifies differential equations and reveals hidden symmetries.
