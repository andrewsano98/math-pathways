


# Organic Chemsitry

## Covalent Bonding and Electron Sharing

Organic chemistry is built on covalent bonds, where atoms share electrons to achieve stability. Molecular structure is determined by electron distribution, electronegativity, and orbital overlap.

--- PAGE ---

## Functional Groups and Chemical Classification

Molecules are organized by functional groups (alcohols, ketones, amines, carboxylic acids, etc.), which determine reactivity patterns. These groups act as predictable “behavior modules” in chemical systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Reaction Mechanisms and Stepwise Transformations

Organic reactions are understood through mechanisms that describe each electron movement step-by-step. Arrow-pushing formalism models how bonds break and form through intermediate states.

--- PAGE ---

## Nucleophiles and Electrophiles

Reactivity is driven by electron-rich (nucleophiles) and electron-poor (electrophiles) species. Reactions are fundamentally interactions of charge density and electron flow.

--- PAGE ---

## Stereochemistry and Molecular Geometry

Three-dimensional arrangement of atoms affects chemical behavior. Concepts like chirality, enantiomers, and stereoisomers show that spatial orientation changes reaction outcomes.

--- PAGE ---

## Resonance and Electron Delocalization

Some molecules cannot be described by a single structure. Resonance models distribute electrons across multiple contributing structures, creating more accurate energy representations.

--- PAGE ---

## Acid–Base Chemistry and Proton Transfer

Acid–base reactions involve proton donation and acceptance. pKa values quantify acid strength, linking structure to equilibrium behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Substitution and Elimination Reactions

Organic reactions often proceed through substitution (replacing groups) or elimination (removing groups to form double bonds). These pathways compete based on conditions and molecular structure.

--- PAGE ---

## Carbon Skeletons and Structural Isomerism

Carbon chains form the backbone of organic molecules. Isomers share formulas but differ in structure, leading to different chemical properties and reactivity.

--- PAGE ---

## Reaction Energy Profiles and Transition States

Reactions proceed through energy barriers and unstable transition states. Reaction diagrams map activation energy and relative stability of intermediates.

--- PAGE ---

## Aromaticity and Stability Rules

Aromatic compounds follow specific electron-counting rules (like Hückel’s rule) that produce unusually stable ring structures due to delocalized electron systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Polymerization and Chain Growth Processes

Small molecules can link into large repeating structures called polymers. This involves chain reactions, catalysts, and controlled growth mechanisms.

--- PAGE ---

## Spectroscopy and Structural Identification

Techniques like NMR, IR, and mass spectrometry are used to determine molecular structure. Each method provides indirect mathematical signatures of bonding and composition.

--- PAGE ---

## Conformational Analysis and Rotational Freedom

Molecules can rotate around single bonds, creating different conformations. Energy differences between conformers determine stability and reactivity.

--- PAGE ---

## Retrosynthetic Analysis and Problem Decomposition

Complex molecules are designed or analyzed by breaking them into simpler precursor structures. This is a reverse-engineering problem-solving method.
