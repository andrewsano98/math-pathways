


# Vector & Tensor Calculus

## Vector Fields as Spatial Structures

Vector calculus studies fields that assign a vector to every point in space, such as velocity fields in fluids or force fields in physics. These encode directional behavior across continuous regions.

--- PAGE ---

## Gradient, Divergence, and Curl Operators

Vector calculus is built on three core operators. The gradient measures steepest change of scalar fields, divergence measures source or sink behavior of vector fields, and curl measures local rotation.

--- PAGE ---

## Conservative Fields and Potential Functions

A vector field is conservative if it can be written as the gradient of a scalar potential. In such systems, work done depends only on endpoints, not the path taken.

--- PAGE ---

## Line Integrals and Work in Fields

Line integrals measure accumulation of a vector field along a curve. In physics, this corresponds to work done by a force along a path through space.

--- PAGE ---

## Surface Integrals and Flux Through Geometry

Surface integrals compute how much of a vector field passes through a surface. This captures flux, which is central in electromagnetism and fluid dynamics.

--- PAGE ---

## Volume Integrals and Distributed Quantities

Volume integrals extend accumulation into three dimensions, allowing computation of total mass, charge, or energy distributed continuously through space.

--- PAGE ---

## Green’s, Stokes’, and Divergence Theorems

These theorems unify vector calculus by relating integrals over regions to integrals over their boundaries. They express deep conservation relationships between local and global behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Differential Forms as Coordinate-Free Structure

Differential forms generalize gradients, curls, and divergences into a unified framework that does not depend on coordinate systems. They encode integration and differentiation abstractly.

--- PAGE ---

## Exterior Derivative and Generalized Differentiation

The exterior derivative extends differentiation to differential forms. It captures how quantities change across dimensions in a way that generalizes all classical vector operators.

--- PAGE ---

## Tensor Fields and Multilinear Structure

Tensors generalize scalars and vectors to objects that encode multilinear relationships. They transform predictably under coordinate changes, making them essential in physics and geometry.

--- PAGE ---

## Rank and Order of Tensors

Tensors are classified by rank, indicating how many directions or indices they involve. Scalars are rank 0, vectors are rank 1, and higher-rank tensors encode more complex interactions.

--- PAGE ---

## Tensor Transformation Laws

A defining property of tensors is how they transform under coordinate changes. Their components adjust in a way that preserves geometric and physical meaning.

--- PAGE ---

## Metric Tensor and Geometry of Space

The metric tensor defines distances, angles, and curvature in a space. It generalizes the dot product and determines the geometry of curved spaces.

--- PAGE ---

## Einstein Summation Convention

Tensor calculus often uses index notation with implicit summation over repeated indices. This compact notation simplifies expressions in high-dimensional systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Covariant and Contravariant Components

Tensor components differ based on how they transform under coordinate changes. Covariant and contravariant forms ensure consistency across different coordinate systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Applications in Physics and Continuum Mechanics

Vector and tensor calculus are foundational in fluid dynamics, elasticity, electromagnetism, and general relativity, where physical laws must hold independently of coordinates.
