


# Quantum Physics

## Wave-Particle Duality and Quantum States

Particles such as electrons and photons behave both like waves and discrete particles depending on how they are observed. Their state is described by a wavefunction that encodes probability distributions. This is a dual-representation physical system.

--- PAGE ---

## Wavefunctions and Probability Amplitudes

A quantum system is described by a wavefunction that assigns probability amplitudes to all possible outcomes. The square of the amplitude gives measurable probabilities. This replaces deterministic prediction with statistical structure. This is a complex-valued probability field system.

--- PAGE ---

## Schrödinger Equation and Time Evolution

The Schrödinger equation governs how quantum states evolve over time. It is a linear differential equation operating on wavefunctions. This allows prediction of how probabilities change dynamically. This is a time-evolution operator system in Hilbert space.

--- PAGE ---

## Superposition and State Combination

Quantum systems can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. These states combine linearly, meaning outcomes are probabilistic combinations of possibilities. This is a vector space superposition system.

--- PAGE ---

## Measurement Problem and Wavefunction Collapse

When a measurement occurs, the system transitions from a superposition of states into a single observed outcome. The exact mechanism is still interpreted differently across quantum theories. This is an observation-induced state reduction system.

--- PAGE ---

## Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Certain pairs of properties, such as position and momentum, cannot both be known precisely at the same time. Increasing precision in one increases uncertainty in the other. This is a fundamental limit on simultaneous measurement precision.

--- PAGE ---

## Quantum Entanglement and Nonlocal Correlation

Entangled particles share linked states regardless of distance. Measuring one instantly determines properties of the other. This does not transmit information faster than light but creates strong correlations. This is a nonlocal correlation system.

--- PAGE ---

## Quantum Tunneling and Barrier Penetration

Particles can pass through energy barriers they classically should not be able to cross. This occurs due to probabilistic wave behavior. It is essential in nuclear fusion and modern electronics. This is a probabilistic barrier penetration system.

--- PAGE ---

## Quantum Numbers and Discrete Energy Levels

Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels defined by quantum numbers. These values determine orbital structure and chemical behavior. Energy is quantized rather than continuous. This is a discretized state space system.

--- PAGE ---

## Pauli Exclusion Principle and Fermion Structure

No two identical fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously. This principle shapes atomic structure and matter stability. It explains electron shell formation. This is a state exclusion constraint system.

--- PAGE ---

## Spin and Intrinsic Angular Momentum

Particles possess an intrinsic form of angular momentum called spin, which is quantized. Spin determines magnetic and statistical behavior of particles. It has no classical analog. This is an intrinsic quantized degree-of-freedom system.

--- PAGE ---

## Quantum Field Theory and Particle Excitations

In quantum field theory, particles are excitations of underlying fields rather than independent objects. Fields exist everywhere in space, and particles are localized energy packets. This is a field-based excitation system.

--- PAGE ---

## Operators and Observable Quantities

Physical quantities like position, momentum, and energy are represented by mathematical operators acting on wavefunctions. Measurement corresponds to applying these operators. This is a linear operator measurement system.

--- PAGE ---

## Eigenvalues, Eigenstates, and Measurement Outcomes

When a quantum operator acts on a state, it produces eigenvalues corresponding to measurable results. Eigenstates represent stable outcomes of measurement. This is a spectral decomposition system in linear algebra.

--- PAGE ---

## Quantum Decoherence and Classical Emergence

Quantum systems lose coherence when interacting with the environment, causing quantum behavior to appear classical. This explains why macroscopic objects do not show superposition. This is an environment-induced transition system.

--- PAGE ---

## Probability Interpretation and Fundamental Uncertainty

Quantum mechanics does not predict exact outcomes but probability distributions over outcomes. Reality at this scale is fundamentally statistical rather than deterministic. This is an intrinsic probabilistic ontology system.
