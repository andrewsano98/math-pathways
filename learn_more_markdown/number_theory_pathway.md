


# Advanced Number Theory

## Prime Numbers and Fundamental Structure

Prime numbers are the building blocks of integers, as every integer factors uniquely into primes. Advanced number theory studies how primes are distributed and what hidden patterns govern their occurrence.

--- PAGE ---

## Modular Arithmetic and Congruence Systems

Modular arithmetic studies numbers under equivalence relations based on remainders. This creates finite cyclic structures that are foundational in cryptography and algebraic number systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Diophantine Equations and Integer Constraints

Diophantine equations seek integer solutions to polynomial equations. These problems are highly constrained and often require deep structural reasoning rather than direct computation.

--- PAGE ---

## Euler’s Totient Function and Multiplicative Structure

The totient function counts integers coprime to a given number. It reveals multiplicative structure in modular systems and is central to many results in arithmetic functions.

--- PAGE ---

## Fermat’s Little Theorem and Congruence Behavior

Fermat’s Little Theorem describes how powers behave modulo primes. It provides fundamental structure for modular exponentiation and underlies modern cryptographic systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Chinese Remainder Theorem and System Reconstruction

The Chinese Remainder Theorem allows reconstruction of integers from modular residues. It decomposes large arithmetic problems into smaller independent systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Quadratic Residues and Reciprocity Laws

Quadratic residues describe which numbers are squares modulo a prime. Quadratic reciprocity reveals deep symmetry between primes in modular square relationships.

--- PAGE ---

## Continued Fractions and Rational Approximation

Continued fractions represent numbers through recursive fractional expansions. They provide optimal rational approximations and connect number theory to dynamical systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Distribution of Primes and Analytic Methods

The distribution of primes is studied using analytic techniques, including complex functions like the Riemann zeta function. This links discrete integers to continuous analysis.

--- PAGE ---

## Riemann Zeta Function and Prime Encoding

The zeta function encodes prime distribution through an infinite product over primes. Its properties connect zeros of the function to deep patterns in prime spacing.

--- PAGE ---

## Prime Number Theorem and Asymptotic Growth

The Prime Number Theorem describes how primes thin out as numbers grow large. It gives an asymptotic estimate for the density of primes among integers.

--- PAGE ---

## Algebraic Number Fields and Extensions

Number fields extend the integers into larger algebraic systems where new notions of divisibility and factorization arise. These structures generalize integer arithmetic.

--- PAGE ---

## Ideal Theory and Unique Factorization Breakdown

In some number systems, unique factorization fails. Ideals restore structure by generalizing numbers into sets that preserve divisibility properties.

--- PAGE ---

## L-Functions and Deep Arithmetic Structure

L-functions generalize the zeta function and encode arithmetic information about number fields, elliptic curves, and modular forms.

--- PAGE ---

## Modular Forms and Symmetry in Arithmetic

Modular forms are highly symmetric functions with deep connections to elliptic curves, partitions, and number theory. They encode hidden structure across arithmetic systems.
