


# Beyond Calculus

## Multivariable Functions and High-Dimensional Domains

Functions extend from one variable to many variables, where inputs exist in higher-dimensional space. Behavior now depends on interactions between multiple directions simultaneously.

--- PAGE ---

## Partial Derivatives and Directional Change

Partial derivatives measure change along individual coordinate directions. Directional derivatives generalize this to arbitrary vectors, capturing how functions vary in any direction in space.

--- PAGE ---

## Gradient Vectors and Steepest Descent

The gradient points in the direction of fastest increase of a function. It encodes local geometry of scalar fields and is fundamental in optimization and physics.

--- PAGE ---

## Jacobians and Multivariable Transformations

The Jacobian matrix describes how multivariable functions distort space locally. It generalizes derivatives to transformations between higher-dimensional coordinate systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Multiple Integrals and Volume Accumulation

Double and triple integrals extend accumulation concepts to higher dimensions. These compute area, volume, and mass over regions in 2D and 3D space.

--- PAGE ---

## Change of Variables and Coordinate Systems

Integration often requires transforming coordinates (Cartesian, polar, spherical). The Jacobian determinant accounts for distortion during these transformations.

--- PAGE ---

## Vector Fields and Spatial Flow

Vector fields assign a vector to every point in space, representing flow systems such as fluid motion, force fields, or velocity distributions.

--- PAGE ---

## Line Integrals and Path Dependence

Line integrals compute accumulation along curves in space. Results may depend on path shape unless the field is conservative.

--- PAGE ---

## Conservative Fields and Potential Functions

A vector field is conservative if it can be expressed as the gradient of a scalar potential. In such cases, path integrals depend only on endpoints.

--- PAGE ---

## Surface Integrals and Flux Measurement

Surface integrals measure how vector fields pass through surfaces. This quantifies flux, which is essential in physics and engineering systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Green’s Theorem and Planar Circulation

Green’s theorem connects line integrals around a closed curve to double integrals over the enclosed region. It links boundary behavior to interior structure.

--- PAGE ---

## Stokes’ Theorem and Higher-Dimensional Circulation

Stokes’ theorem generalizes circulation concepts to surfaces in 3D space. It connects curl of a vector field to its behavior along boundaries.

--- PAGE ---

## Divergence Theorem and Volume Flux Balance

The divergence theorem relates flux through a closed surface to behavior inside the volume. It formalizes conservation principles in multivariable systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Curl and Local Rotation

Curl measures rotational tendency of a vector field at a point. It captures local swirling behavior in fluid and electromagnetic systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Multivariable Optimization and Constraint Systems

Optimization in higher dimensions involves finding maxima and minima subject to constraints. This uses gradients, Lagrange multipliers, and geometric reasoning.

--- PAGE ---

## Lagrange Multipliers and Constrained Extremization

Lagrange multipliers allow optimization under constraints by balancing gradients of objective and constraint functions.

--- PAGE ---

## Differential Forms and Coordinate-Free Calculus

Differential forms generalize integration and differentiation in a way that is independent of coordinate systems, unifying many multivariable calculus concepts.
