


# Theoretical Biology

## Population Dynamics and Differential Equations

Theoretical biology models population changes using differential equations. Birth rates, death rates, and resource constraints determine growth curves such as exponential and logistic models.

--- PAGE ---

## Lotka–Volterra Systems and Predator–Prey Models

Interacting species are modeled using coupled nonlinear differential equations. Predator–prey dynamics produce oscillations that reflect feedback loops in ecological systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Evolutionary Game Theory

Evolutionary game theory applies strategic interaction models to biological fitness. Traits persist based on payoff structures rather than conscious decision-making, leading to stable or unstable equilibria.

--- PAGE ---

## Fitness Landscapes and Adaptive Peaks

Evolutionary processes are visualized as movement across fitness landscapes. Populations tend to drift toward local maxima, though stochastic effects can lead to exploration of alternative peaks.

--- PAGE ---

## Genetic Drift and Stochastic Evolution

Not all evolutionary change is deterministic. Random sampling effects in finite populations introduce stochasticity, modeled using probability theory and Markov processes.

--- PAGE ---

## Systems Biology and Network Modeling

Cells and organisms are modeled as networks of interacting components. Gene regulatory networks, protein interactions, and metabolic pathways form complex dynamical systems.

--- PAGE ---

## Reaction Networks and Biochemical Dynamics

Biological reactions inside cells can be represented as coupled reaction systems. These networks are analyzed using rate equations and steady-state behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Morphogenesis and Pattern Formation

Organismal development involves spatial pattern formation governed by chemical gradients and reaction–diffusion systems. This explains structures like stripes, spots, and segmentation.

--- PAGE ---

## Allometric Scaling Laws

Biological properties often scale predictably with size. Relationships between mass, metabolism, and lifespan follow power laws derived from physical and geometric constraints.

--- PAGE ---

## Epidemiological Modeling

Disease spread is modeled using compartmental systems such as SIR models. These use differential equations to describe transitions between susceptible, infected, and recovered populations.

--- PAGE ---

## Coevolution and Interaction Dynamics

Species evolve in response to each other, creating coupled adaptive systems. This introduces feedback loops across multiple evolutionary timelines.

--- PAGE ---

## Information Theory in Biology

Biological systems encode and transmit information. DNA, neural signaling, and ecological communication can be analyzed using entropy and information transfer metrics.

--- PAGE ---

## Stability Analysis and Equilibrium Behavior

Theoretical biology often focuses on whether systems reach stable equilibria or exhibit oscillatory or chaotic behavior under perturbations.

--- PAGE ---

## Agent-Based Models and Emergent Behavior

Complex biological patterns can emerge from simple rules applied to many interacting agents. This connects micro-level rules to macro-level behavior.

--- PAGE ---

## Robustness and Biological Constraints

Living systems maintain function despite noise and perturbations. Robustness is studied as a structural property of networks and feedback systems.
